### Thesis (the stock-vs-flow error)
Consensus trades Hormuz "normalisation" symmetrically across the energy complex. It is wrong in
one specific place: natural gas. Crude normalises when tankers sail; **destroyed liquefaction
capacity does not**. Mid-March Iranian strikes destroyed Ras Laffan trains S4 and S6 plus a GTL
facility — 12.8 mtpa, ~17% of Qatari exports, offline **for years** (replacement turbines on 2–4
year lead times; QatarEnergy has declared force majeure to buyers in China, Korea, Italy and
Belgium). Meanwhile EU storage is 40.4% vs a 55.0% five-year norm, with injection pace
insufficient to hit the mandated 90% by 1 November. Yet Goldman's 2027 TTF forecast is €23 and
the back curve charges only ~€5.5/MWh of winter-26/27 premium. The curve prices a flow problem;
the physical reality is a stock problem (storage hole) compounded by a capacity problem
(destroyed trains). Sell-side desks and producer Cal-27 hedging are the price-insensitive flow
flattening the back end.

### Asymmetry (haircut from the research agent's numbers)
